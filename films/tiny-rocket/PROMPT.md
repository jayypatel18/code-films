# TINY ROCKET: the prompt

Written in the format of the prompt [@chetaslua](https://x.com/chetaslua) published for "THE LAST PAGE", with a new story.

---

Create a 2D anime short film for the web. It must look completely like real Japanese anime, as if it were taken directly from a finished theatrical anime film. This is traditional-looking, hand-drawn 2D anime, not a 3D animation, a moving illustration, a stylized web graphic, or a tech-company product ad.

Deliver the final result as a single, self-contained HTML file with sound. Use any tools, libraries, references, guides, literally anything. Pre-rendering the film and embedding it in the file is allowed. All artwork and audio must be original. The page should be a black cinema frame with one quiet play button (browsers block autoplay with sound) and nothing else on screen.

Use Studio Ghibli films as the art style reference for the design, line work, painted backgrounds, color, and atmosphere, without copying any existing character or shot. No people appear in the film. The main character is an original young red panda, a small tinkerer who has never left its hill. Use any tools or techniques you want, including 3D for layout, as long as the final result looks 100% like hand-drawn 2D anime.

THE FILM: "TINY ROCKET" (about 60 seconds, 24 fps)

The idea: All summer, a small red panda has been building a rocket out of paper and scrap. Tonight it finds out whether a homemade dream can reach the sky.

Shot 1, The Last Knot (0:00-0:05). Open directly on the red panda, close, in warm dusk light. Its paws pull a knot tight with a quick tug while it holds the end of the string in its teeth. Its ears flick, its striped tail swishes, and it blinks at its work, pleased with itself. The audience sees a living character moving in the very first frame.

Shot 2, The Hill (0:05-0:12). Wide. A grassy hilltop above a river valley at dusk, one old camphor tree leaning over it, a little village far below where lights come on one by one along the river, the first stars. On the hilltop stands the rocket, as tall as three red pandas: rolled paper and old tin, a teakettle nose cone, bamboo legs, one patched sailcloth fin, stars painted on by paw. The panda steps back to admire it, then checks a hand-drawn star map pinned to a stick.

Shot 3, Waiting for Morning (0:12-0:19). Night. The panda sleeps curled against the rocket under a patchwork blanket while the sky turns slowly overhead with thousands of stars. A single shooting star crosses. One ear twitches in its sleep. Fireflies drift in the grass.

Shot 4, First Light (0:19-0:25). Blue before dawn. It wakes, climbs into the cockpit (a big teacup with a round window), and pulls down a pair of oversized goggles. Close-up: whiskers, a nervous swallow, then a small determined nod.

Shot 5, The Spark (0:25-0:31). It strikes a match. The fuse sparks across the grass toward the rocket on hard held key poses. Close-up: the panda squeezes its eyes shut and holds its breath. One beat of total stillness. Nothing happens. It opens one eye.

Shot 6, Lift (0:31-0:37). Ignition. A burst of painted smoke and sparks, grass and leaves flattened outward in a ring, the rocket shudders, then leaps into the air. Low angle as it climbs past the camphor tree trailing smoke, and a flock of small birds bursts from the branches.

Shot 7, Through the Clouds (0:37-0:44). The rocket punches up through layers of cloud catching the first sunrise. Paper fins rattle. Inside the teacup, the panda's cheeks are pushed back by the wind, then its face turns from fear to wonder as it breaks into open sky.

Shot 8, The Shooting Star (0:44-0:52). This is the climax. Seen from far away, high above the whole valley at dawn, the rocket's trail ignites into a brilliant shooting star that arcs across the sky over mountains, a sea of clouds, rivers catching the light and the curve of the world. Make this the widest, most awe-inspiring image in the film.

Shot 9, Home (0:52-1:00). Quiet. Morning on the hilltop. The rocket drifts down under an umbrella parachute and settles in the grass. The panda climbs out, sooty-faced, goggles askew, and looks up at the sky where one star is still fading. It takes out the star map and paints a tiny new star on it with its paw. Then it looks at us, and its tail curls once. The title brushes in over the sky in ink:

Tiny Rocket

Below it, a small red hanko seal reading 星 presses into the sky like a stamp on paper, followed by one line in smaller brushed lettering with the name of the model that made the film.

Hold on the title. Fade to paper, then black. Do not add logos, feature lists, benchmarks, or any product claims.

ANIME QUALITY STANDARD

Every frame must look as if it was drawn by professional anime animators and painted by master background artists for a finished theatrical film.

Red panda: A hand-drawn anime character with a simple, appealing, solid design, cel-colored forms, and clear silhouettes: russet fur, a white face mask with red tear marks under the eyes, big white-rimmed ears, dark legs, a thick ringed tail. Keep it on-model from every angle, and let its ears, tail, posture, and eyes express emotion without any words.

The rocket: Hand-drawn with real handmade character, including paper seams, tape, dents, rivets, paint drips, and patches. It must keep a consistent design from shot to shot.

Line art: Confident, delicate, hand-inked anime line work with subtle variation in thickness and pressure.

Coloring: Traditional anime cel coloring with clean color areas, simple hard-edged shadows, and a soft, natural palette.

Backgrounds: Richly detailed, hand-painted anime backgrounds in a gouache style, with visible brushwork, natural color variation, layered depth, and individually considered grass, trees, roofs, and clouds.

Lighting: The film travels from warm golden dusk to deep starry blue night, through cold blue first light, to an overwhelming golden sunrise above the clouds. Use colorful shadows, glowing mist, drifting fireflies and sparks, and soft atmosphere that unite characters and background.

Animation: Authentic hand-drawn anime timing, mostly on twos and threes, with real weight, strong key poses, natural holds, smears on the fastest frames, and overlapping movement in fur, grass, smoke, paper, and leaves. The film should build slowly, then release with explosive power.

Direction: Cinematic anime shots, beautiful composition, thoughtful staging, and pacing that starts with a small living moment, builds tension, and ends in a soaring climax, followed by one small, tender moment.

Text: All text must look brushed in ink and appear only in the final shot, with no web fonts sitting on top of the picture.

SOUND

Create an original score and sound design, with no borrowed melodies. Start with evening crickets, a soft breeze, and the creak of the knot, then a lone felt piano motif as the panda admires its rocket. Add a music box or celesta color under the night sky. Bring in a ticking, held-breath tension for the fuse, then true silence for the beat when nothing happens. Then a roar of ignition, rattling tin and paper, and a full orchestral swell as the rocket breaks through the clouds. At the climax, the piano motif returns at full scale. End with a single soft piano note as the paw paints the new star. Sound effects should include the knot, paws in grass, the match strike, the fuse fizz, the ignition, birds taking flight, wind, the parachute, and the tiny brush stroke.

IT MUST NOT LOOK LIKE

- 3D: No plastic materials, CGI shading, visible polygons, game-engine lighting, or computer-perfect perspective.
- SVG or vector art: No simple geometric shapes, perfect curves, uniform outlines, flat corporate illustration, or basic gradients.
- Web animation: No sliding layers, constant bobbing, floating parts, smooth software easing, or puppet-like joint rotation.
- AI art: No warped shapes, melted details, flickering, garbled text, or changing character designs.
- Non-anime art: No Western cartoon designs, realistic rendering, soft painterly shading on the characters, or generic digital illustration.
- Tech advertising: No glowing neural networks, circuit patterns, chat bubbles, UI mockups, futuristic HUDs, particle-logo reveals, or typed-out text effects.
- Amateur work: No stiff poses, empty backgrounds, repeated trees or roofs, identical stars or sparks, muddy colors, clip art, placeholders, or unfinished details.

You are free to change any part of this prompt if it would produce a better result.

The result should feel brave, warm, and full of wonder, like the first time you believed you could do something impossible. Before finishing, inspect random frames as still images from every shot. If any frame does not look completely like real 2D anime, keep improving it. Do not stop until every frame could pass as a still from a theatrical anime film.
